The People's Perspective

Friend's mother, Yang, 2024:

Yang is the eldest daughter in her family. They lived in a small town where there was a strong preference for boys. At the time, the policy allowed couples to have a second child if the first had a serious disability.

“What my family did was take me for an eye exam and told me in advance that no matter what the doctor asked, I should just say I couldn’t see. So, I went in for the eye test, and the vision chart was placed in front of me. At first, I followed the instructions and said I couldn’t see. But when the doctor said, ‘You're a good girl and so pretty. Here's an apple for you,’ and asked me again, suddenly, I could see everything. It was such a funny story. My mom said that’s why we didn’t get the disability certificate.” — Guo Yang, 2024

A friend who worked at a hospital used his connections to help Yang’s parents secure a birth permit, allowing their baby boy to be born in the hospital and receive a birth certificate. However, when it was discovered that they had a second child, both parents were fined the equivalent of a year’s salary by their employers.

Yang’s husband, Chao (meaning "exceed"), was a second child who almost didn’t survive. His mother was relentlessly pursued by family planning officials to terminate the pregnancy. Since he was born at home, his parents couldn’t obtain a birth certificate for him. They eventually managed to get him registered in the household months later through unofficial channels. As a result, his recorded birthdate is incorrect. Once the birth was discovered, his father was not only fined about a year’s salary but was also demoted at his job.

Yang shares the dramatic, yet not uncommon, story about her husband’s birth:

Nanfu Wang, director of One Child Nation, interviewed someone who helped her brother abandon his baby daughter so he could try again for a son.
“We couldn’t discard the baby in broad daylight. So we carried her in a basket, climbed over mountains when it was still dark out. We put $20 in her clothes and left her on the meat counter in the market. For two days and two nights, she was there. No one wanted her. Her face was full of mosquito bites. She eventually died. Then we buried her.” — One Child Nation, 2019

Official's Perspective

Again, from One Child Nation, Nanfu Wang interviewed a high standing official, Shuqin Jiang.
“If I could go back in time, I would do this work again. Looking back, the policy was absolutely correct. Our leaders were prophetic. If not for this policy, our country would have perished. I was only 19 when I started working in family planning. I initially thought that forcing abortions was an atrocity. I wanted to quit several times. But the leaders said to me, ‘It is a national policy and as a party member, the more challenging the job, the more determined you should be to take it on.’ Many of the fetuses aborted were eight or nine months along. When they were aborted, they were still alive. Sometimes pregnant women tried to run away. We had to chase after them. One time a pregnant woman was so distraught, she took off all her clothes and ran away naked. Unable to catch her, we turned to the Party Secretary and asked, ‘Where should we grab her? There’s nothing to grab onto!’ That’s how things were. During abortions, women would cry, curse, fight, go insane… Those memories… I had to put the national interest above my personal feelings. It was like fighting a war. Death is inevitable. It really was like that. We were fighting a population war.” — Shuqin Jiang, 2019
