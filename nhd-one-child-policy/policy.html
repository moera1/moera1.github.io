1979: The Beginning

The groundwork for the One-Child Policy was laid in 1979 by Deng Xiao-ping. He believed that for China to advance as an economy, the population needed to be under control.

Courtesy of Wikimedia

"We encourage one child per couple. We give economic rewards to those who promise to give birth to only one child." — Deng Xiao-ping, 1979
"The one child family policy was developed and implemented in response to concerns about the social and economic consequences of continued rapid population growth." — National Library of Medicine, 1999

1984: Amendments to the Policy

Around 1984, exceptions were introduced, allowing rural couples to have a second child if their first child was a girl, due to a strong preference for male children to help with agricultural work. Couples could also apply for a second child if their first child had a disability that prevented them from becoming independent.

1990s-2000s: Strict Enforcement

Throughout the 1990s and the 2000s, the policy was strictly enforced, with significant penalties, forced sterilizations, and abortions for non-compliance.
"I really don't know how many [babies] I delivered. What I do know is that I've done a total of between 50,000 to 60,000 sterilizations and abortions... But I had no choice; it was the government's policy." — Huaru Yuan, 2019

2015: Policy Termination

As Deng Xiaoping anticipated, having the population under control helped the Chinese economy climb. However, there were fewer youths to take the jobs. Finally, in 2015, in response to the negative effects of an aging population and labor shortages, the government officially ended the one-child policy, replacing it with a two-child policy.
And in 2021, the government began encouraging families to have up to three children. Later that year, all limits on the number of children were removed, as the government sought to boost the declining birth rate to keep up with the rising economy.
